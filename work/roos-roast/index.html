Roos Roast

A mobile-first redesign for an Ann Arbor coffee roaster, built to make buying beans easier.

My role
UX Designer
Team
Pair project with Dylan Siewert
Timeline
January to April 2024
Tools
Figma, Google Sheets
View mobile prototype View desktop prototype

Overview

Roos Roast is an Ann Arbor coffee staple known for quirky branding and high quality beans. Its Shopify site made the main task, buying coffee, harder than it needed to be.

The challenge and the solution

The challenge

  • Checkout was cumbersome. The Shopify checkout created friction for people trying to finish a purchase.
  • Beans were hard to find. Buying beans is the main goal, but the path from the home page wasn't intuitive.
  • The brand didn't come through. Great photos and artwork, but no strategy for using them to tell the Roos Roast story.

The solution

  • A shorter, branded checkout flow
  • Made bean discovery and variety easy to see
  • A new Leave a Note feature that brings the community's personality onto the page

The constraint: A quirky brand, a standard web

Roos Roast is a fun, quirky place with a big personality. Carrying that personality onto a website, while still meeting today's standards for usability and visual design, was the hardest balance to strike.

Results

  • Top 6of 40 teams presented to the Roos Roast owners
  • 2prototypes, mobile and desktop, designed mobile first
  • 3core flows redesigned: home, product, checkout

Design solutions

Home page

  • Oversized logo
  • Small text
  • Chunking that made the flow unclear
  • Focus on locations and subscriptions to drive engagement
  • Community notes add Roos personality with a purpose

Checkout flow

  • Oversized logo
  • Long scroll to complete checkout
  • Unclear purchase confirmation
  • Checkout chunked into clear steps
  • Branded checkout experience
  • Clear order confirmation page

Product page

  • No way to change roast or bean type on the page
  • No clear focal point for the text
  • Small font size
  • Added roast type selection
  • Consistent dropdowns for every option
  • Larger, more readable font

Process

Visiting the roastery

Our class visited the Roos Roast roasting facility and met founder John. As longtime Roos customers, my partner Dylan and I wanted to do the brand justice.

Planning the work

I set up a lightweight project plan in Google Sheets so we could track progress without constant check-in messages. It paid off when we met for a big design sprint.

Sketching and wireframing

We each sketched independently to start with a wider range of ideas, then designed mobile first and iterated through several rounds of wireframes.

What I learned

  • Prioritize the client's asks. Clients often have many requests. Ranking them led to a more focused design and process.
  • Personality needs color. Seeing other teams' work, I realized our design could carry more of the brand's personality with bolder color. With more time, that's where I'd go next.
